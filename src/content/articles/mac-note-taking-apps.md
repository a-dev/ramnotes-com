Most notes apps would like to be the only one you ever need. In practice, many of us end up with two: a notebook for the things we want to keep, and a quick spot for the thought that only has to survive until lunch. Apple Notes is a lovely notebook if you use a Mac and an iPhone. For the quick spot, even the Stickies app that came with your Mac can do the job.

I built RAMnotes for that second spot, so I have a favorite on this list, and I'll try to be fair about it. Below are eleven other apps I'd happily recommend to a friend, what each one is good at, and the things they do that RAMnotes never will.

<aside data-note data-tone="yellow">
<p data-label>The short version</p>

If you want a notebook that follows you to your iPhone, start with Apple Notes. For a big library of linked notes, Obsidian is hard to beat, and for a team, Notion is the usual home. If you want a few notes one shortcut away, saved as plain text files in folders you choose, that's the job RAMnotes was made for.

</aside>

## Built-in Mac apps and floating notes

### RAMnotes

Press Control + Backslash in any app and your notes appear, with the note you used last ready for typing. Press the same shortcut again, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, and they politely step out of the way. Each note can have its own tint and text size, so the green one for Project A and the big one for today's call are easy to tell apart. If you'd like your notes to stay above other windows, there's an Always on top setting for that.

Every note is a plain `.txt` file. A list of release tasks can live in the project folder while your shopping list sits in iCloud Drive, and any text editor or script with access to the folder can read and change the same file. RAMnotes works offline, needs no account, and runs on Apple Silicon Macs with macOS 27 or later.

### Apple Notes

Apple Notes is the notebook already waiting on your Mac, iPhone, and iPad. A recipe note can hold a photo, a checklist of ingredients, and a link to the page where you found it. With iCloud turned on, you can tick things off at the store or share the list with whoever is doing the shopping.

For quick capture, press Globe-Q and a Quick Note opens over whatever you're doing. All of those notes land in one Quick Notes folder, which feels tidy at first and gets crowded after a few busy weeks. RAMnotes is the better fit when a note belongs in a particular folder, right next to the project it's about.

### Stickies

Stickies has been on the Mac since 1994, and it still lives in your Applications folder. Type a reminder, choose Float on Top from the Window menu, and the note stays visible while you work in another app. Colors and translucency help it stay readable without covering much of your screen.

For a phone number you need until the end of a call, Stickies may be all you need. RAMnotes keeps the same sticky-note spirit and adds a shortcut that shows or hides every note at once, a searchable note list, and a separate text file for each note.

### Raycast Notes

If you already open apps with Raycast, give Raycast Notes a hotkey and you'll get a floating editor right above whatever you're working on. You can write Markdown, keep a checklist, and jump between notes without leaving the keyboard. Your notes stay together inside the launcher.

Raycast is an easy choice if you like formatting and already have the launcher installed. RAMnotes makes more sense when a colleague or a script needs the file in a particular folder.

## Scratchpads and quick capture

### Tot

Tot gives you seven colored dots, each with room for text, and that's the whole idea. When all seven are full, you have to decide which one to clear, which is a gentle nudge if your scratchpads tend to turn into attics. The dots sync through iCloud, so a packing list you start on the Mac is waiting on your iPhone at the airport.

Tot and RAMnotes share the idea of a few short-lived notes close at hand. Tot keeps the whole set in one window and has an iPhone app. RAMnotes has no seven-note limit and lets you spread notes across separate windows.

<dl data-facts>
<dt>Price</dt><dd>Free to download, $19.99 for the full Mac app</dd>
<dt>Notes live in</dt><dd>Seven text spaces inside Tot, synced with iCloud</dd>
<dt>Pick it if</dt><dd>you like a friendly limit and want the same notes on your iPhone</dd>

### Antinote

Antinote is especially good at scratch work with numbers. Press Option-A and type a calculation or a unit conversion, and the result stays right next to the figures, so later you can still see how you arrived at the total. You can pin a note to keep it, or let it expire on its own.

Antinote stores its notes in a local database and can export them as text. If you often reach for a calculator halfway through a note, it's a delightful little tool. RAMnotes has the advantage when you want the text file in your folder from the first keystroke.

### Drafts

Drafts opens to a blank page, ready for text, on your iPhone or your Mac. Its real strength shows up after you've typed. An action can turn a draft into an email or send it to another app, so you skip the usual round of copying and pasting.

That makes Drafts a lovely inbox for text that belongs somewhere else. RAMnotes suits text you want to keep in view while you work, such as questions for a call or the commands you're about to run.

## Writing, notebooks, and shared workspaces

### Bear

Bear is a Markdown writing app for Apple devices, and it's a pleasant place to spend an afternoon. You can start with a paragraph and organize it later with nested tags, so a draft about growing herbs can sit under both gardening and cooking and you'll find it either way. Bear Pro adds iCloud sync across your devices.

Bear is a good home for notes you come back to over several sittings, or for browsing your writing by subject. For a few lines that only need to last until lunch, a floating RAMnotes window is probably closer to hand.

<dl data-facts>
<dt>Price</dt><dd>Free tier, Bear Pro for sync</dd>
<dt>Notes live in</dt><dd>Tagged notes inside Bear</dd>
<dt>Pick it if</dt><dd>you want a calm place to write and keep notes on Apple devices</dd>
<dt>Website</dt><dd><a href="https://bear.app/">Bear</a></dd>
</dl>

### Obsidian

Obsidian keeps Markdown files in a folder it calls a vault. Link a meeting note to a decision, and months later you can follow that link to see what the team agreed on. Because the files are ordinary Markdown, you can also open them in any text editor.

Obsidian and RAMnotes both leave you with the original files. Obsidian is the place for a large library where the links between notes are worth keeping, and RAMnotes is for a few independent notes, each in the folder where you'll use it. Plenty of people use both: they write in RAMnotes during the day and let a script or an AI task move what matters into the vault later.

<dl data-facts>
<dt>Price</dt><dd>Free, with an optional paid Sync service</dd>
<dt>Notes live in</dt><dd>Markdown files in a vault folder</dd>
<dt>Pick it if</dt><dd>you want a long-term, linked knowledge base in plain files</dd>

### Notion

Notion puts documents, databases, and wikis into one shared workspace. A project decision can live on the same page as the specification, where a teammate can find it, ask a question, or fix the wording.

Notion needs an account and keeps your pages on its servers, which is exactly what a team wants. For your own rough notes during a meeting, RAMnotes lets you write locally first and decide afterward which details deserve a place on the project page.

## Plain-text alternatives across devices

### Simplenote

Simplenote suits the list you write on a Mac and read on an Android phone or a Windows laptop. Sign in to the same account and the text syncs everywhere, including a web app. Tags help you find an older note without remembering its title.

Simplenote is the one to pick when reaching your notes from different operating systems matters most. RAMnotes is a Mac app, and you decide which files, if any, go into a synced folder.

<dl data-facts>
<dt>Price</dt><dd>Free</dd>
<dt>Notes live in</dt><dd>Your Simplenote account</dd>
<dt>Pick it if</dt><dd>you need free plain-text sync on Windows, Android, or the web</dd>
<dt>Website</dt><dd><a href="https://simplenote.com/">Simplenote</a></dd>
</dl>

### FSNotes

FSNotes is an open-source notes manager that works directly with Markdown and plain-text files. You can browse a folder of notes, search the text, and edit offline. Keep the folder in iCloud Drive and it shows up on your iPhone as well.

FSNotes and RAMnotes both suit people who want to keep their files, and the difference is in how you work with them. FSNotes gives you a library to browse in one main window, while RAMnotes gives you small windows you can arrange around the work in front of you.

## Where RAMnotes fits, and where it doesn't

Since I made RAMnotes, here is my honest list of both sides.

<div data-split>
<div data-note data-tone="green">
<p data-label>What it does well</p>

- One shortcut shows your notes from any app, and the same shortcut hides them. You can also scroll over the menu bar.
- The note you used last is ready for typing as soon as your notes appear.
- Each note has its own tint and text size, so green can mean Project A and big can mean "today".
- Each note is a `.txt` file in any folder you like, including iCloud Drive, Dropbox, or a project folder.
- Scripts and AI tools can read and edit a note in a project folder, and you'll see their changes the next time you open it.
- It needs no account and no network access, and the whole app is smaller than 4 MB.

</div>
<div data-note data-tone="red">
<p data-label>When another app will serve you better</p>

- There's no iPhone or iPad app, though you can open the text files in the Files app.
- Notes are plain text, so there are no images, checklists, or formatting. That's on purpose, but it's still a limit.
- There are no tags or notebooks, so it isn't built to hold a thousand notes.
- Sync comes from the cloud folder you choose, not from RAMnotes itself.
- There's no sharing or collaboration.
- It needs an Apple Silicon Mac with macOS 27 or later.

</div>
</div>

## Frequently asked questions

<details data-faq>
<summary>Which free Mac app can keep a note on top of other windows?</summary>

Stickies comes with macOS. Open a note and choose Float on Top from the Window menu. RAMnotes is free too: turn on Always on top in its settings, and use the global shortcut to show or hide all your notes at once.

</details>

<details data-faq>
<summary>Which Mac notes apps save plain files?</summary>

RAMnotes saves each note as a `.txt` file in a folder you choose. Obsidian uses Markdown files in a vault, and FSNotes manages folders of plain-text and Markdown files. All three let you open the original text in another editor without exporting it first.

</details>

<details data-faq>
<summary>Is RAMnotes a replacement for Apple Notes?</summary>

Probably not, and it doesn't try to be. Apple Notes is for notes you keep on all your devices, and RAMnotes is for short-term notes on your Mac. They get along well side by side.

</details>

<details data-faq>
<summary>Can I use RAMnotes with Obsidian?</summary>

Yes. Keep a RAMnotes window open for meeting questions, then copy the answers worth keeping into a Markdown note in your Obsidian vault. If you automate that step, your script can read the RAMnotes text file directly.

</details>

<details data-faq>
<summary>Can I read RAMnotes on my iPhone?</summary>

Yes. Save a note in iCloud Drive, then open its text file in the Files app on your iPhone. If you'd like the same notes app on both devices, Apple Notes, Tot, Bear, and FSNotes have native iPhone apps.

</details>

<details data-faq>
<summary>Is RAMnotes free?</summary>

Yes. The Mac app is open source under the MIT license, and the [GitHub release](https://github.com/a-dev/ramnotes/releases) is free. The App Store version is the same app, so buying it is simply a way to say thanks. It runs on Apple Silicon Macs with macOS 27 or later.

</details>
